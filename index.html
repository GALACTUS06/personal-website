About Me

When I'm not coding…

You can find me at Lenovo innovation forums, testing the latest hardware, or co-designing interactive demo stations in flagship stores. I love translating real user pain points into better technology.

A curious builder who connects technology, research, and people.

My journey started with a simple habit: taking things apart to see how they work. That curiosity grew from PC hardware diagnostics into programming, and eventually into research on how machines understand people — from detecting social groups in egocentric video to generating lifelike crowd animations in virtual cities.

Today, I work in Stony Brook's Synthetic Perception and Learning Lab, where I contribute to EgoGroups, a large-scale benchmark for detecting social groups in the wild, and I lead an independent subproject that brings event-driven storytelling to CARLA/OpenMap city environments. Along the way, I have built agentic financial-analysis pipelines on AWS Bedrock and data-driven web applications deployed with GitHub Actions.

What drives me is the space where rigorous systems meet human experience. Whether I am annotating video frames, scripting walking-tour renders, or designing a web interface, I care about work that is reproducible, accessible, and genuinely useful to the people who interact with it. I am currently open to research collaborations, internships, and team projects in AI, computer vision, and interactive media.

Research Experience

Summer 2026

Outdoor Event-Driven Storytelling on CARLA / OpenMap City Stages

Independent Summer Research Subproject  ·  Advised by Prof. Paola Cascante-Bonilla

  • Constructed outdoor city stages from CARLA/OpenMap assets exported as USD tiles, and adapted walkable maps to keep agents safely on sidewalks.
  • Extended Event-Driven Storytelling (EDS) to CARLA-derived environments by mapping prompts to structured social events and generating sidewalk-constrained trajectories through motion matching.
  • Developed a fully scripted demonstration pipeline in which coding agents authored, debugged, and retargeted crowd layouts — baking walk, chat, hug, and wave segments into reproducible Python walking-tour renders.
  • Demonstrated multi-agent films across diverse city tiles under tour, commute, and gathering layouts, improving motion diversity and realism while reducing collisions and clipping artifacts.

EgoGroups — A Large-Scale Dataset for Detecting Groups of People in the Wild

Synthetic Perception and Learning Lab  ·  Advised by Prof. Paola Cascante-Bonilla

  • Contributed to EgoGroups, a large-scale dataset capturing social group behavior from walking-tour videos recorded in cities worldwide.
