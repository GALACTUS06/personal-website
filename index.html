About Me

From opening up a PC to building research tools

I once opened up a computer to add a hard drive. Taking things apart to see how they worked got me interested in troubleshooting PCs, then in programming and computer vision research.

At Stony Brook's Synthetic Perception and Learning Lab, I work on EgoGroups, where we study how people form groups in walking-tour videos. I also design simulated walking tours with Social Tour Simulator, which lets me choose what different cameras can observe.

Publication

In EgoGroups, we use first-person walking-tour videos from cities around the world to study how models tell which people are together. We compare language and vision-language models with supervised baselines and report how detection performance varies with crowd density and region.

Jeffri Murrugarra-Llerena, Pranav Chitale, Zicheng Liu, Kai Ao, Yujin Ham, Guha Balakrishnan, and Paola Cascante-Bonilla (2026). EgoGroups: A Benchmark for Detecting Social Groups of People in the Wild. arXiv preprint arXiv:2603.22249.
